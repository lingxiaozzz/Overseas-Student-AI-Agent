# Student Health, Wellbeing, and Emergency Support

This guide provides safe, general support pathways for international students in Sydney. It does not replace medical, emergency, counselling, or legal advice.

## Everyday wellbeing

- Adjusting to study, housing, language, and a new city can be stressful; seek support early rather than waiting for a crisis.
- Use appropriate university wellbeing, counselling, disability, academic, and international student support services.
- Keep your OSHC policy details, emergency contacts, and relevant medical information accessible.
- For non-urgent health concerns, use an appropriate health service and check how your OSHC policy applies.

## Urgent situations

- If there is an immediate threat to life or safety, contact emergency services first.
- If you are on campus, also use the university's current campus-security or emergency guidance when it is safe to do so.
- If you feel at risk of self-harm or are unable to stay safe, seek urgent professional help and contact emergency or crisis-support services.
- Tell a trusted person where possible; do not stay alone with an immediate safety risk.

## After an urgent event

- Seek medical or wellbeing support, even if the incident initially seems manageable.
- Notify the relevant university support team if the event affects attendance, assessment, accommodation, or safety.
- Keep any documents that may be needed for special consideration, insurance, or support requests.

Use current official university and local emergency information for phone numbers, service hours, and campus procedures.
